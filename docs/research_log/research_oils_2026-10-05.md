## Q5. Fatty-acid profiles of Indian oils from non-IFCT source

Only the NIN/IFCT-team paper "Fatty acid profile of edible oils and fats consumed in India" (Food Chemistry 2017; 320 samples: oils 236, vanaspati 45, ghee 39) surfaced, SNIPPET_ONLY via academia.edu. Printed values: coconut oil total SFA 90.84%; safflower highest PUFA (76.78% in abstract; 75.58% in an AI takeaway, conflict); groundnut oleic 53.77%; soybean linoleic 54.17%; mustard erucic 48.5-54.2%. This is likely the same NIN dataset that feeds IFCT 2017, so it is probably NOT independent. No SFA/MUFA/PUFA table for the oils of interest was seen.

## (c) Conflicts / cautions
- Production shares (USDA; FE: mustard 40%) differ strongly from consumption shares (SEA: palm 33.4%, mustard 15.6%) because palm and soy are mostly imported. Do not mix.
- SEA consumption excludes ghee/butter (1-1.5 kg/capita) and industrial use, while per-capita 17 kg/yr is edible oil only. USDA consumption totals (23.1 MMT MY23/24, 26.3 MMT MY24/25) differ slightly from SEA (23.5-24 MnT) in definition.
- Cow ghee SFA 73.71% vs buffalo 68.17% (Gujarat study) conflicts with the common belief that buffalo ghee is more saturated (commercial blog claim, SNIPPET_ONLY); the study's own result is as printed.
- Safflower PUFA 76.78% (abstract) vs 75.58% (AI takeaway) in the same academia page.
