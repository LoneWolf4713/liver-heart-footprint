### Interpretation (per the handoff guide)
- Takeaway: ratios 0.47-0.88 on both metrics (every takeaway dish below 1) -> restaurant/takeaway food carries more fat than the web home recipes imply; consistent with the home-vs-restaurant setting in the app. Caveat: biryani and prawn bhuna lab rows are UK takeaway averages and the recipes are home-style; chicken biryani's lab fat (7.4 g/100 g) also includes chicken fried in oil, which this check was not meant to cover.
- Home: NOT ~1. Saag and (to a lesser degree) methi aloo agree (0.8-0.9); bhindi, sambar and vegetable pilau are over-estimated 1.2-2.8x. The recipes call for 1-3 tbsp oil/ghee for small quantities (e.g. 3 tbsp for 250 g okra), whereas the Leeds samples (5-6 samples each; how they were prepared is not stated in CoFID) contain less fat. A plausible but UNTESTED explanation is that blog quantities are not what the sampled households used. This is the opposite sign to the takeaway finding, so there is no single correction factor.
- Overall coverage is low and the dispersion large -> report plainly: ingredient sums are a reasonable first approximation of the order of magnitude, not a validated point estimate.

## 5. Limitations
- n is small: 9 distinct dishes, 2-4 recipes each; only the 5 home dishes come from one UK lab survey (Leeds 2007, n=5-6 samples each, ingredient list unknown). Ratio dispersion between recipes of the same dish (e.g. M1 9.1-19.2 for sambar) is as large as the recipe-vs-lab gap.
- Ground truth is UK-made South Asian food (Leeds; takeaway averages across UK outlets), not Indian food in India; recipes are mostly Indian-blog recipes. A mismatch in preparation is possible in every pair.
- Ingredient nutrient data are UK CoFID, not IFCT (varieties of spinach/okra/methi/dal differ); piece and cup weights are 8% my assumption and 31% INDB Units.xlsx (e.g. medium onion 60 g, tomato 50 g), tested only in the sensitivity runs.
- Fat is assessed by sums, not cooking losses/uptake (fat left in the pan, oil absorbed by fried components); dry-matter and %-energy metrics remove only the water problem. M3 (fat per 100 g as eaten, assumed 60-80% moisture) is in results.json for reference only and is not used for conclusions.
- Chen, Gilbert & Khokhar 2009 (source C) was not accessible (Zenodo record restricted) -> NOT AVAILABLE; its abstract says recipe-calculated values exceeded analysed values for most nutrients but differences were smallest for fat/protein/carbohydrate (not used as data).
- safefood values are mean portion fat / mean portion weight, not mean of per-sample ratios; kcal/fat 'M2' for safefood is the ratio of means.
- CoFID rows that look like home dishes but are recipe-calculated (e.g. 'Curry, lentil...homemade', 'Recipe ... Updated 2014') were excluded; the analysed-vs-calculated decision rests on the Description/Main-data-reference fields (the Notes sheet does not define them).
- Not tested: whether the app's frying/swap logic works; only whether ingredient sums approximate lab fat for non-fried dishes. Status of the claim 'ingredient sums predict cooked fat': **partly tested, inconclusive** (ratio 0.5-2.8, direction setting-dependent).
