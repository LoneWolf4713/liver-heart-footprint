Deliverable 2 (D2) · Pipeline Architecture & Methodology

The 5-Stage Footprint Engine & Clinical Grounding

A deterministic, multi-tiered computational pipeline that standardizes vernacular Indian recipe inputs, resolves physical gram densities, maps ingredients to empirical IFCT 2017 nutrient profiles, corrects for deep-frying oil absorption artifacts, and calculates clinical metabolic load indices.

Stage 01 src/lhf/parse.py
Input Standardization

Multi-Dialect Ingredient & Unit Parser

Isolates quantities, standardizes 50+ vernacular culinary units, extracts parenthetical metrics, and strips non-nutritive culinary adjectives.

Online and regional Indian recipes use non-standardized units (katori, chammach, pinch, clove, bunch, cup, tsp) and heterogeneous phrasing. The parser applies deterministic normalization:

  • Unicode Fractions & Ranges: Evaluates fraction characters (½ → 0.5, ¼ → 0.25) and written words ("one", "two"), collapsing ranges ("1-2 tsp" → 1.5).
  • Parenthetical Metric Priority: Detects explicit masses embedded in descriptors (e.g. "chicken (500g, bone-in)" overrides volumetric assumptions with 500g).
  • Culinary Noise Stripping: Removes cutting and preparation modifiers ("finely chopped", "roasted", "for tempering", "to taste") via compiled regex before food token extraction.

Stage 02 src/lhf/grams.py
Mass Standardization

Deterministic Density & Mass Resolution

Converts culinary volumes, piece counts, and vernacular household measures into precise physical gram weights (g).

Nutrient tables operate exclusively on mass per 100 grams, but human home cooks measure in cups, spoons, and counts. This stage maps volumetric inputs to grams using calibrated bulk density factors:

  • Calibrated Physical Densities: Liquid cooking oil has a specific density of 0.92 g/ml; 1 cup maida settles at 120g, while 1 cup tur dal weighs 175g and 1 cup diced paneer weighs 150g.
  • Multi-Tiered Measure Resolution: Checks measures.csv via a 3-tier cascade: (Code, Unit) exact match → (Food Group, Unit) group fallback → (Global, Unit) fallback.
  • Unit Size Granularity: Distinguishes piece counts by qualifier (e.g. "1 medium onion" → 60g, "1 large onion" → 110g, "1 clove garlic" → 3g).

Stage 03 src/lhf/match.py
Nutritional Ontology

High-Confidence IFCT 2017 Matching

Maps cleaned culinary names to verified clinical nutrient profiles in the Indian Food Composition Tables (1,003 food components).

Raw vernacular names rarely match clinical chemistry nomenclature (e.g. "paneer" is cataloged as "Cottage cheese" under code E018). The matching engine uses an authoritative 3-tier cascade:

  • Tier 1 · Curated Aliases (Confidence 1.0): Instant lookup across 800+ expert-verified mappings in aliases.csv resolving colloquial terms (jeera → cumin, methi → fenugreek, dalda → vanaspati).
  • Tier 2 · Tokenized Normalized Substring (Confidence 0.95): Strips remaining noise words and matches against indexed IFCT common names and scientific taxonomy.
  • Tier 3 · Conservative Levenshtein Matching (Confidence ≥ 0.85): Fuzzy string similarity with strict threshold cutoff (0.85) to prevent spurious lipid cross-assignment.

Stage 04 · Critical Innovation src/lhf/frying.py
Empirical Lab Correction

Deep-Frying Immersion Bath Correction

Eliminates the massive recipe inflation artifact caused by pan immersion oil baths using Soxhlet laboratory uptake assays.

When a home cook fries Samosas, the recipe routinely lists "500 ml oil for deep frying" (460g). Standard nutrition tools naively calculate 460g of fat, yielding an impossible 4,000+ kcal per serving! In reality, diners consume only the fraction absorbed into the crust:

  • Automated Bath Detection: Scans for lines classified as COOKING_OIL or HYDROGENATED_FAT exceeding 50g accompanied by frying triggers ("for deep frying", "to fry").
  • Bath Stripping: Completely excludes the entire 460g immersion volume and its lipid load from the raw recipe sum.
  • Empirical Uptake Injection: Looks up the food matrix in fried_foods_lab.csv (Gupta et al. 2016 Soxhlet lab extractions) and injects actual absorbed fat based on finished solid mass: Absorbed Fat = (non-oil mass / 100) × lab_fat_g_100g.

Stage 05 src/lhf/footprint.py
Clinical Risk Translation

Dual-Organ Metabolic Scoring (HLI & CBI)

Translates per-serving nutrient intakes into concrete organ strain indices against ICMR-NIN 2024 and WHO daily physiological ceilings.

Standard nutritional reports list raw grams without clinical context. The footprint engine standardizes nutrient intake per portion (÷ servings) against daily metabolic ceilings for an urban Indian adult (2,240 kcal/day baseline):

  • Hepatic Load Index (HLI): Quantifies liver steatosis and de novo lipogenesis risk driven by Saturated Fatty Acids (ICMR ceiling: 24.9 g/day) and Free Added Sugars (WHO ceiling: 25.0 g/day).
  • Cardiovascular Burden Index (CBI): Quantifies atherogenic and arterial vascular strain driven by Saturated Fat (24.9 g), Total Dietary Fat (74.7 g / 30% energy), and Sodium (1,967 mg / 5g salt).
  • Crossroads Metabolic Bottleneck: Identifies whether Saturated Fat, Sugar, or Sodium represents the critical biological driver forcing clinical lifestyle adaptation.

Provenance, Ground Truth & Model Boundaries

Empirical Datasets, Clinical Baselines & Transparent Limitations

Full academic bibliography, analytical lab methods, acknowledged computational boundaries, and course team credits.

Empirical Data Sources & Academic Bibliography

1. IFCT 2017 (ICMR-NIN): Indian Food Composition Tables, National Institute of Nutrition. Contains 1,003 primary food components with proximate and lipid profile assays.

2. INDB: Indian Nutrient Databank. Standardized corpus of 1,014 traditional recipes providing our culinary baseline.

3. ICMR-NIN 2024: Dietary Guidelines for Indians. Establishes SFA ceilings (<10% general, <5% high-risk), total fat ceiling (30% energy), and 2,240 kcal urban adult baseline.

4. Gupta et al. (2016): Soxhlet lipid extraction and fatty acid profiling of cooked foods and street snacks available in Delhi.

5. UK CoFID (2021): Composition of Foods Integrated Dataset. Proximate lab values for ethnic restaurant and takeaway portions.

6. Neethu et al. (2025): Controlled deep immersion frying trials of traditional milk-solid sweets (Gulab Jamun / Pantoa) in ghee vs vegetable oil.

7. ICMR-INDIAB (Nature Medicine 2025): Cross-sectional epidemiological study (n=18,090) reporting national SFA consumption patterns.

Acknowledged Model Limitations & Boundary Conditions

• Confection Frying Mass Distortion: In syrup-soaked sweets (Gulab Jamun, Jalebi), finished food mass includes heavy sugar syrup, which inflates the mass multiplier used in absorbed fat re-injection. Affects ~5% of corrected confections.

• Unquantified Culinary Salt: Recipes listing "salt to taste" lack explicit gram weights. Sodium values for these dishes represent strict empirical lower bounds derived from inherent ingredient sodium.

• Pot-Level INDB Soup Coding: Soups & Stews in INDB are coded at the preparation batch level rather than per-bowl service size, causing anomalous sodium figures in the raw atlas.

• Schema.org Web Scraper Coverage: URL scraping requires structured JSON-LD (schema.org/Recipe). Supported on major recipe portals (~70% compliance across Indian recipe sites).
