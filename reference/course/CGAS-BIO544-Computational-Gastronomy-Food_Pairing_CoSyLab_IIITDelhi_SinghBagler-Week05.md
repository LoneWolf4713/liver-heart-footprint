## Data - driven Investigations of Culinary Patterns in Traditional Recipes across the World

Navjot Singh1,2 &amp; Ganesh Bagler 1

1 Center for Computational Biology, Indraprastha Institute of Information Technology, Delhi 2 Delhi Technological University

## Prologue

The knack for combining and processing raw ingredients has been suggested to be critical for the evolution of large brains.

The penchant for cooking has led to diverse cooking styles across cultures which are crystallized as elaborate procedures in traditional recipes.

Passed down the generations, these recipes form the core of cultural identity and shape the dietary habits.

## Motivation

While it is clear that preparation and perception of food are complex phenomena that make it difficult to determine underlying rules, search for patterns in recipes can provide much-needed impetus in developing the scientific foundation .

Analysis of data pertaining to recipes, ingredients and relevant features can help uncovering underlying patterns in traditional recipes, paving way for innovation in food design and flavor pairings.

## Bird's Eye View

We present a framework for multi-level investigation of traditional recipes rooted in the annotated data of recipes from diverse world cuisines and those of flavor molecules from natural ingredients .

Systematic investigation of deviation patterns from random compositions provide insights into 'culinary fingerprints' that can form the basis for synthesis of novel recipes as well as targeted alterations in existing recipes.

## Data of Flavor Molecules

![Image](/mnt/c/Users/prern/Downloads/Academics/CGAS/Full_Lectures/CGAS-BIO544-Computational-Gastronomy-Food_Pairing_CoSyLab_IIITDelhi_SinghBagler-Week05_figures/image_000010_80dd731c5996953300688b4e06fbf6d8096e9a8ef6eccb470df0edddc3d76f0f.png)

FlavorDB comprises of 25595 flavor molecules among which 2254 are associated with 936 natural ingredients. However, this list was tailored for the analysis of recipes:

-  29 generic and noisy entities were removed.
-  For certain ingredients, their common names were added.
-  Linguistic and spelling variations of ingredients were accounted for.
-  13 new ingredients for which flavor profiles were available but were coarsegrained in FlavorDB were added due to their role in recipes.
-  Data from Ahn et al., was used to include 4 more unique ingredients.
-  Information for 7 additives was manually added considering their high usage.

## Compound Ingredients

In addition to 840 unique basic ingredients, we compiled a list of 103 'compound ingredients'. This list includes:

-  Readymade spice combinations, sauces and other commonly used dishes which are often considered as core ingredients themselves.
-  Some entities from FlavorDB were bundled to create a compound ingredient to account for limited number of flavor molecules in certain ingredients.

## Recategorization

FlavorDB's ingredient categorization was inherited, however recategorization was done to reduce number of categories. Therefore, eventually each of the 942 ingredients was classified into one of the 21 categories.

## Shortcomings

-  Recipes attributed to a particular cuisine at any of the online source(s) might not be "traditional" per se.
-  While there is a large number of ingredients in the database, it is far from exhaustive.
-  Ingredients not identified by aliasing protocol are labelled for manual curation.
-  Same recipe might be submitted at multiple sources, no real way to eliminate redundancies.
